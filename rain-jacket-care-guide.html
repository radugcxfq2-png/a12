The Jacket Care Guide

A waterproof jacket that is washed and re-treated when it needs it will work for many years. One that is never washed will seem to fail in two. Here is the routine.

Why washing helps, not harms

Many people avoid washing a rain jacket for fear of damaging it. The opposite is true. Sweat, skin oils, sunscreen and dirt clog the membrane and smother the water-repellent finish. A dirty jacket breathes poorly and wets out quickly. Washing clears the pores and often revives beading without any further treatment.

As a rule of thumb, wash after every ten to twelve days of wear, or whenever water stops beading on the shoulders and cuffs.

Before you start

  • Read the care label. It overrides anything written here.
  • Empty the pockets and brush off dried mud.
  • Close all zips and fasten flaps and cuff tabs. Loosen drawcords.
  • Run the detergent drawer under the tap to clear old powder and softener.

Washing, step by step

1

Use the right cleaner. A liquid made for technical outerwear, or pure soap flakes. Avoid biological detergent, fabric softener and bleach; all leave residues that attract water.

2

Choose a gentle cycle at 30°C, or the temperature on the label, with a slow spin. Wash the jacket alone or with one or two similar items.

3

Rinse twice. An extra rinse removes the last traces of cleaner, which matters more than anything else for beading.

4

Dry as the label allows. If tumble drying is permitted, twenty minutes on low heat helps reactivate the factory finish. Otherwise hang it to dry, then iron on the coolest setting through a towel.

The splash test

When the jacket is dry, flick a few drops of water onto the shoulder. If they sit as round beads and roll away, you are done. If they spread and soak in within a few seconds, the repellent finish has worn out and needs replacing.

Reproofing

There are two kinds of treatment. Wash-in products go through the machine and coat everything, inside and out; they are easy and even, and suit unlined shells. Spray-on products are applied to the outside only, which is better for jackets with a wicking lining, and lets you concentrate on shoulders, cuffs and the hood.

  1. Start with a freshly washed jacket. Treatment will not bond to dirt.
  2. Apply while the fabric is still damp, following the product instructions exactly.
  3. Wipe away any drips or pooling with a cloth.
  4. Dry with gentle heat if the label allows; most treatments cure better warm.

Storage and small repairs

Hang the jacket on a wide hanger in a dry, airy place. Do not leave it compressed in its stuff sack for months, which creases and weakens the membrane. Small tears can be sealed with a self-adhesive repair patch, rounded at the corners, applied to clean dry fabric. Peeling seam tape can sometimes be pressed back with a cool iron through baking paper; if it lifts again, a repair service can re-tape it.

When a jacket is finished

If the inner face is flaking, the membrane is breaking down and no treatment will restore it. Until that point, washing and reproofing will keep almost any jacket working. The Reproofing Reminder estimates when yours is next due.
