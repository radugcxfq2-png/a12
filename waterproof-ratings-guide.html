The Waterproof Ratings Guide

A rain jacket’s label is a row of numbers and abbreviations. Each one answers a simple question, and once you know the questions the label reads like a sentence.

Waterproof, water resistant, water repellent

These three terms are used loosely, and they mean different things.

  • Water repellent describes a surface finish that makes water bead. It is a treatment, not a barrier, and it wears off.
  • Water resistant describes tightly woven fabric that sheds light rain for a while. Under pressure or in sustained rain it lets water through.
  • Waterproof describes fabric with a membrane or coating that stops liquid water, joined with sealed seams.

A jacket is only as waterproof as its seams. Waterproof fabric with unsealed stitching will leak along every needle hole.

Hydrostatic head

The test is simple. A tube is placed on the fabric and filled with water until drops push through. The height of the water at that moment, in millimetres, is the rating. Sitting, kneeling and rucksack straps press water against fabric much harder than falling rain does, which is why the numbers needed are larger than you might expect.

Breathability

Breathability is usually given as grams of water vapour passing through a square metre of fabric in twenty-four hours. A figure of 5,000 suits standing about; 10,000 to 15,000 suits steady walking; 20,000 or more suits fast, sweaty effort. Some makers quote a resistance figure called RET instead, where lower is better and anything under 6 is very breathable.

No membrane keeps up with hard uphill work. That is what vents are for.

Two, two-and-a-half and three layers

2-layer

Membrane bonded to the outer fabric, with a separate hanging lining. Comfortable and quiet, a little bulky. Common in everyday jackets.

2.5-layer

Membrane bonded to the outer, with a thin printed or sprayed protective layer inside. Very light and packable; can feel clammy on bare skin.

3-layer

Outer, membrane and inner lining bonded into one sheet. The most durable and the most breathable in use. Stiffer, and usually the most expensive.

Seams and zips

Fully taped means every seam has waterproof tape heat-bonded over it on the inside. Critically taped means only the most exposed seams, usually shoulders and hood, are sealed. For anything beyond a shower, choose fully taped. Zips should be either laminated water-resistant zips or ordinary zips covered by a storm flap.

Putting the numbers together

For a daily commute and weekend walks, 10,000 mm, 10,000 g and fully taped seams is the sensible baseline. Go higher on the first number if you carry a heavy pack, and higher on the second if you run hot. The Waterproof Rating Decoder will read any label for you.
